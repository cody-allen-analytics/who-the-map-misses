# Ethics & data sovereignty

## Studying the algorithm, not the communities

This project audits a tool and the institutions that use it. It does not build a new instrument
for profiling Indigenous communities, and it does not publish rankings of specific localities as
candidates for or against programs.

## Principles followed

The project follows the **CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance** (Collective benefit,
Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) alongside FAIR data practice:

- **Collective benefit:** the question is whether public tools under-serve Indigenous communities,
  and the main output is evidence that can correct that.
- **Authority to control:** only public, aggregated government statistics are used. The census
  categories are the state's, not the communities' own; that limitation is stated wherever results appear.
- **Responsibility:** findings are framed around map and institutional error, not community deficits.
  Synthetic results are always labeled as synthetic.
- **Ethics:** no individual or household data, no re-identification risk, no locality-level outputs committed.

## Language

- "Indigenous" is capitalized; specific peoples are named where the data allow.
- Results describe *places classified by census measures*, not people's identities.
- Deprivation is described as lack of services the state should provide, not as a trait of a community.

## Known limits of the categories

Household- and language-based census measures undercount people who self-identify as Indigenous,
and they treat diverse peoples as one group. Results by people or region would need data the
public census tables do not provide.

## Feedback

If you are part of, or work with, an affected community and see a problem with how this work
represents it, please open an issue or contact the author. Corrections will be logged.
